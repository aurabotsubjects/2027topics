# The Royal Road — Persia (Years 5–6)

*Quest for the Seal of the Kings.* A teacher-run presentation app for a 10-week term on ancient Persia. Every lesson is 50 minutes, and each day is a different subject: History — the Persians and the biblical history of Persia (Mon), Geography — settlements and cities (Tue), Science — animal communities and habitats (Wed), Technology — desert engineers, then Royal Road messages and coding (Thu) and Art — art of the empire (Fri). It is built on the same engine as *The Fifth Codex* and *The Braided Map*, re-themed in lapis, gold and clay. The full plan is in `Persia_App_Blueprint.md`.

**Status:** Phase 1 (engine and look) and **all ten weeks** are built: fifty full lessons with scripted slides, interactives, lesson plans, rubrics, Path Cards and sources. The old app in `../Persia` is untouched.

**Decisions used:** the blueprint's recommended defaults for D1–D14 (name *The Royal Road*; teacher-run, no logins; Thursday Technology and Friday Art; reused images; Nasrin and Master Hamid as the new guides; young earth first with a labelled 🔬 side note; no political commentary on Iran today). Change any of them and the next build will follow.

## Open it
- **On your Mac:** double-click `index.html`. It runs in Chrome or Safari and works offline (fonts are included).
- **Online:** upload this folder to a GitHub repo (e.g. **`persianew`**), then turn on GitHub Pages or connect it to Netlify. There's no build step.

## Present
Go to **Week 1**, choose a day, then press **Present from start**.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| → / Space / clicker | Next slide or next reveal |
| ← | Back |
| N | Teacher notes and scripts (SAY / ASK / LISTEN FOR / IF THEY SAY… / 🕊️ CARE / 🔬 SIDE NOTE) |
| T | Timer |

## The weekly mystery
- Every Mon–Thu lesson ends with a **Waystation Clue** (a number). Type it on the keypad; solved clues are saved.
- Friday ends with the **Seal Fragment lock**: add the four clues, write the total in **Old Persian number signs** (1, 2, 10, 20, 100, biggest first) and press them into the clay. The clay cracks and the fragment flies to its place on the Seal of the Kings. Week 1: 3 + 2 + 4 + 3 = **12** = 10 + 2. Week 2: 45 + 25 + 3 + 10 = **83** = 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 2 + 1. Week 3: 3 + 111 + 4 + 14 = **132** = 100 + 20 + 10 + 2. Week 4: 300 + 11 + 71 + 0 = **382** = 100 + 100 + 100 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 2. Week 5: 14 + 27 + 45 + 2 = **88** = 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2. Week 6: 52 + 42 + 3 + 5 = **102** = 100 + 2. Week 7: 5 + 54 + 2 + 3 = **64** = 20 + 20 + 20 + 2 + 2. Week 8: 330 + 85 + 17 + 11 = **443** = 100 ×4 + 20 ×2 + 2 + 1. Week 9: 3 + 25 + 90 + 4 = **122** = 100 + 20 + 2 (and the Shadow is unmasked: Zal). Week 10: 21 + 7 + 30 + 5 = **63** = 20 + 20 + 20 + 2 + 1, then the Forgiveness Vote and the Seal's message (from Proverbs 2:4 and 2:6).
- Then **the Shadow's Note** raises a question that runs all term. Week 3's note is in code (a Caesar shift of 3: *THE ROAD IS LONG*); students crack it in Week 7 Technology.
- The Seal on the home page fills in as fragments are found.

## Lesson plans, rubrics and PDFs
Each lesson page has a **Lesson Plan PDF** (ERO-ready, with 2027 curriculum links, 🕊️ care notes and a Sources & evidence page), **Rubric PDF** (Developing 🌱 / Achieved 🪙 / Flying 🪶 with student-work examples), **Student Rubric**, **Path Cards (A · B · C)** and **Quick-Mark**. Choose **Print / Save as PDF** for the best quality.

Every Week page ends with **Sources & further reading**, grouped by day and labelled by type (Museum, Government, University, Encyclopaedia…). Week 1–3 🔎 facts were checked in October 2026 (new for Weeks 2–3: Te Ara on the capital city, WWF on the grassland animals, Britannica on satraps, Livius on the Apadana stairs, Biola University on Darius the Mede, DOC on Predator Free 2050). In Week 1 the one claim that couldn't be verified (a qanat cooling a windcatcher's air) was left out. To add a source, edit `data/sources.js`.

## Before you teach
- Read the 🕊️ care notes on Monday's plan: some students may have family links to Iran or the Middle East (invite, never require, sharing), and Iran today is taught without political commentary.
- The Old Persian number signs are simplified wedge shapes for the classroom; the slide notes link a chart to compare.
- The maps and globe are simplified (places and coastlines approximate).
